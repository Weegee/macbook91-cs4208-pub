# Incident 2026-10-19: a headphone plug stops a running capture

**Status: open in 20261015.01 and every earlier release. A driver fix is in the
tree; it is not yet measured on hardware.**

## What happened

During a voice call on the MacBook9,1 that used the internal microphone, the maintainer
plugged a headset (a four-pole plug) into the combined jack. The call audio
moved to the headphones, but the microphone stopped recording. Unplugging did
not bring it back, and KDE's microphone test, which names the device as the
internal microphone, detected no sound. The microphone came back only after
all audio had stopped for a while.

## Measured on the test machine, 2026-10-19

20261015.01 on kernel 7.2.11-1-cachyos. Everything ran without root and with
plain ALSA on `hw:0,0`, so PipeWire is not part of the failure. Nothing was
recorded: the captures wrote to `/dev/null`, and their progress was read from
`/proc/asound/card0/pcm0c/sub0/status`. A silent four-channel playback kept the
codec awake throughout.

| step | capture | converter 0x06 |
| --- | --- | --- |
| capture A starts, headphones out | runs, `hw_ptr` advances | stream=1 |
| headset plugged | A ends with Input/output error | stream=0 |
| fresh capture B | opens, `hw_ptr` stays 0 | stream=0 |
| headset unplugged | B still stalled | stream=0 |
| fresh capture C | opens, `hw_ptr` stays 0 | stream=0 |

The playback kept running, and `alsactl monitor` saw three Headphone Jack
events and no Mic Jack event: the four-pole plug was not detected as a headset,
so the capture source stayed the internal microphone.

During the call itself, a capture opened on `hw:0,0` got no data and converter
0x06 read stream=0. After the call had ended and the codec had runtime-suspended,
a new capture received data (768000 bytes) and 0x06 read stream=1. Comparing
the codec in those two states, the internal microphone's path (pin 0x19 to
converter 0x06) differs only in that stream tag; its amplifier values are
identical.

## Mechanism

1. A plug makes `a1534_hp_jack_callback()` replay `headphones_a1534()`, the
   vendored headphone sequence (origin: `vendored/PROVENANCE.md`). It takes the audio function group to D3
   and back eight times (`tests/run-tests.sh` section 7e counts them) and
   afterwards writes the stream tag of the playback converter 0x02 only.
   Converter stream tags do not survive those cycles: 0x06 read stream=0 after
   the plug, and in the runner's codec-state capture after a plug on
   2026-10-06, converter 0x0a read stream 0 while keeping its format, although
   nothing in the sequence writes its stream tag.
2. The HDA core caches what it last wrote to each converter.
   `snd_hda_codec_setup_stream()` skips the write when the cached stream tag
   and format match, and the cleanup at the end of a capture is sticky: it
   keeps the cached values. A later capture gets the same stream tag, so the
   core never writes the converter again, and that capture stays silent.
3. Only a runtime suspend of the codec drops the cache
   (`hda_cleanup_all_streams()`), which is why the microphone came back once
   all audio had stopped.

Steps 2 and 3 are read from the kernel source (`sound/hda/common/codec.c`,
7.2.11) and match every observation above. Step 1 rests on the two
observations of a lost stream tag, not on a register trace of the D3 cycles.

## Why the earlier PASS was wrong

`regress.concurrent_capture` was recorded as PASS on 2026-09-15 and 2026-10-06
from `tools/macbook91-runner jackstream`. In both runs the jack read `on` at the
runner's first poll after its prompt: the headphones were already plugged in,
and the runner never checked the state it started from. Only the unplug was a
real transition, and an unplug replays `play_a1534()`, which has no D3 cycle. A
plug during a capture had never been exercised.

## The fix

`src/cs420x-a1534/a1534_pcm.h` re-programs the capture converter past the
core's cache: a cleanup with `do_now` set forgets the cached values, and the
setup that follows writes the stream tag and format again
(`a1534_capture_resync()`). It runs

- after every replay in `a1534_hp_jack_callback()`, for a capture running
  across the plug, under the HDA bus `prepare_mutex` and never inside
  `st->lock`;
- on every capture prepare (`spec->gen.pcm_capture_hook`), for a capture
  opened after the plug.

The generic HDA parser swaps converters under a running capture the same way
(`dyn_adc_pcm_resetup()`). The runner's `jackstream` waits for the jack to read
the opposite state before each prompt and restarts the capture after each
transition, and `macbook91-audio-verify` keeps a capture to `/dev/null` running
across its headphone plug and unplug.

## Not covered

- The headset microphone itself. The four-pole plug is not detected as a
  headset, and the replayed sequence sets the headset microphone pin 0x18 to
  `VREF_HIZ`, without bias: it read so during the call. Calls keep using the
  internal microphone. `docs/HEADSET-MIC.md` collects what is known and the
  probe that would decide it.
- Pins and widgets outside the internal microphone's path were not compared
  for capture purposes.

## Workaround without the fix

Stop every stream for a few seconds: end the call or close the applications
that use audio. The codec then suspends, and the next capture works.
